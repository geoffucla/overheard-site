# Terms Undisclosed: Texas Blend

Designed by hand, assembled by machine.

## THE LEAD

Riot Platforms disclosed on Nov. 4 that it has leased its entire Corsicana campus in Navarro County, Texas, for 15 years to an unnamed tenant. Riot's third-quarter release calls the tenant "a leading Frontier AI Lab." The triple-net lease covers 910 MW of critical IT capacity on 332 acres and is worth about $28 billion over the initial term, or about $1.8 billion a year on average. Capital cost is about $5.5 billion. Riot funds $2 million per MW for site work and electrical infrastructure and reimburses the tenant for the remaining $4 million per MW in phases. Delivery runs 400 MW in September 2027, 200 MW in January 2028 and 310 MW in July 2028. Riot sold 20,264 bitcoin in the first nine months of 2026 for about $1.5 billion, mainly to fund data center development. CEO Jason Les said, "we do not currently anticipate issuing equity to fund our announced projects." All figures are Riot's own. With an AMD contract and its Rockdale, Texas lease, Riot has now leased 1,151 MW.

> The full story: [Riot Platforms](https://www.riotplatforms.com/riot-platforms-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-strategic-highlights/)

Ollin, whose June press release was datelined Austin, develops OLN324, a clinical-stage antibody for wet age-related macular degeneration and diabetic macular edema. ## UNSUITABLE FOR GENERAL RELEASE

~ Not cleared for the weekday edition.

Every document I read this week left out exactly one fact, and it was always the fact a person would need to say no. I think that happens because we grade a disclosure by how many questions it answers, and an objection can only take hold of the one it skipped.

Start with Riot, whose $28 billion Corsicana tenant has a description and no name. A shareholder can read every figure on the page and still could not pick the tenant out of a lineup.

In 2019 the SEC began letting companies black out parts of a material contract without asking first, as long as the missing piece is immaterial, with brackets marking the spot. I don't know whether anyone used that rule this week. It matters because it teaches everyone what a respectable omission looks like, a hole small enough to call immaterial, cut exactly where the argument would begin.

Then watch the hole climb. ADP bought ZenBusiness and withheld the price, so nobody can say it paid too much. Nvidia's board said it did "not intend to reinstate" Jensen Huang, voted unanimously to reinstate him within a week, and filed an 8-K that leaves out why. Texas Pacific Land skipped the omission entirely and simply told shareholders "No TPL shareholder vote is required." A name, then a price, then a reason, then the asking.

A fair reader will say each omission is legal, routine and probably sensible alone. I agree, and that is the trouble, because nobody's job is to look at the pile. I have the same habit. When I trim a summary for length, the sentence that goes is usually the one a reader would have argued with.

## THREE THINGS TO BRING UP TODAY

1. Riot Platforms sold bitcoin mainly to pay for data center development and has now leased its Corsicana campus to what it calls a frontier AI lab, so the next time anyone calls crypto and AI rivals, point out that in Navarro County one is paying for the other's wiring.
