## THE LEAD

Fort Worth-based Range Resources agreed Oct. 28 to be acquired by EQT, of Canonsburg, Pennsylvania, in an all-stock deal valued at $13 billion. The combined company would be run from EQT's Canonsburg headquarters, so Fort Worth would lose a public company. EQT puts the combined enterprise value above $50 billion and says the deal would make it the country's largest natural gas producer, with daily net production above 9 billion cubic feet of natural gas equivalent. EQT holders would own roughly 73% of the combined company and Range holders 27%. Range President and CEO Dennis Degner and one other Range representative would join EQT's board after closing, expected in the first quarter of 2027 pending regulatory and shareholder approvals. Range employs about 500 people between Fort Worth and the Appalachian Basin and said it won't change roles or responsibilities for now. Neither company has said what happens to the Fort Worth headquarters or its employees.

> The full story: [Dallas Innovates](https://dallasinnovates.com/fort-worth-based-range-resources-to-be-acquired-by-eqt-in-13b-all-stock-deal/)

Disruptive Technology Advisers, $10 billion, Fund. The Dallas venture firm backs growth- and late-stage companies in AI, AI infrastructure, and defense and national security. Its Disruptive Flagship Fund is its largest and is described as the largest in Texas, with sovereign wealth funds, family offices and institutions among the investors. Warhound, $200 million, Seed. The Dallas startup plans to combine Colossal Biosciences' genetics and cloning technology with Anduril's defense gear to produce military working dogs, including Belgian Malinois and German Shepherds. It emerged from stealth at a reported $1.2 billion valuation, with backers including CAZ Investments of Houston, 8VC of Austin, Sequoia Capital and In-Q-Tel. It was co-founded by Colossal CEO Ben Lamm and Anduril co-founder Palmer Luckey, and its first CEO is David Spirk, a Marine Corps veteran. ## THE SCUTTLEBUTT

~ What is reportedly being said, and by whom.

Texas Parks and Wildlife drafted a closed-door agenda item on the "disposition" of Cameron County land, according to the Texas Tribune, and Texas Monthly reported the land was purportedly for SpaceX. The item was pulled from the agenda, and the department says it is "not considering the sale of the Boca Chica property at its November meeting." Gov. Greg Abbott said a sale would not come to a vote "anytime soon." The department has asked the Attorney General to keep the draft private under the "deliberative process" exemption, which protects deliberations about a sale it is not considering.
