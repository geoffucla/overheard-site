## THE LEAD

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company, through a U.S. unit, filed an application Monday under the Texas Comptroller's JETI program for a proposed fab in Brazos County. The site is just over 3,200 acres in College Station's extraterritorial jurisdiction, with parcels under contract but not purchased. The plant would make logic chips at 2 nanometers or more advanced for AI, high-performance computing and mobile. The estimated investment is $22.6 billion, with at least 2,099 direct jobs at an average annual salary of $133,000. TSMC seeks a 10-year school district property tax limitation, which the governor and the College Station ISD board must approve after the Comptroller reviews it. The district vote could come as early as mid-December. TSMC has made no final investment decision, and Brazos County is competing with Sherman in North Texas and with Arizona. County Manager Trevor Lansdown said, "We are very excited about this potential transformational opportunity for our citizens." TSMC holds about 70% of the global contract chipmaking market, WTAW reports, so where it builds next matters to Texas.

**UP: Axiom Space.** The Houston company signed a memorandum of understanding on Nov. 19 with Kansas-based Nitride Global to grow aluminum nitride semiconductor crystals in microgravity, where Nitride says they can form with fewer defects. No financial terms were disclosed, so this is a statement of intent, though one built on earlier NASA Ames work and useful to a company developing a successor to the International Space Station. Lucie Low, Axiom's chief science officer, wants to "demonstrate real commercial value in low-Earth orbit (LEO)," and the value remains undisclosed.
