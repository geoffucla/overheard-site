## THE LEAD

Corpus Christi has spent at least $50 million on a seawater desalination plant it never built, and abandoning the project could require repaying $235 million in state loans plus about $70 million in interest. Industrial users including OxyChem, Valero, Citgo and Flint Hills Resources began discussing a plant with the city in 2014. In July 2018 the council unanimously adopted an exemption fee, about an 8% rate hike, that spared them drought surcharges that could exceed 100% of their rates. It averaged about $6 million a year, against estimated debt service of about $68 million a year. The cost estimate climbed from $140 million in 2019 to $1.2 billion in July 2025, and the council fired the contractor that September. Council Member Mark Scott said, "That's going to be a painful experience if the state of Texas calls our notes due." A November ballot measure would repeal the fee and raise industrial water prices in future droughts. Its result will set who pays for water in the next dry spell.

> The full story: [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/12/corpus-christi-desalination-water-drought-industry/)

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, steeped plainly.

The Texas Energy Fund has finalized its ninth loan, for a 452-megawatt natural gas plant in Reeves County. Gov. Greg Abbott announced it Oct. 7, and the loan amount was not disclosed. Pecos Power Plant LLC is developing the project with Mercuria and Continental Resources as co-sponsors, and it is expected to supply the ERCOT grid in 2027. A Continental Resources filing shows its affiliate held a 50% economic interest as of June 30, had contributed $164 million, and has capital commitments of up to $257.8 million, including that amount. With Pecos, the nine projects total 5,446 MW, against 4,994 MW for the first eight. ERCOT's unofficial 2026 peak of 91,089 MW on July 22 compares with 85,508 MW in 2023. PUC Chairman Thomas Gleeson called the plant "a dependable source of power for Texas families and businesses."

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

Century Communities announced Thursday that it acquired substantially all assets of HistoryMaker Homes, a Grapevine builder that began in Fort Worth in 1949, builds only in DFW and has built about 25,000 homes. Price was not disclosed. The deal triples Century's DFW community count, and the two operations will merge into one Grapevine-based division.
