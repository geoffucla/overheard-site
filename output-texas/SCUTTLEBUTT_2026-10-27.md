## THE LEAD

EQT agreed Monday to buy Fort Worth-based Range Resources in an all-stock deal, and the combined company will remain headquartered in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania, under EQT's management team. Each Range share converts into 0.987 EQT shares, leaving EQT shareholders with about 73% of the combined company and Range shareholders about 27%. Pro forma enterprise value exceeds $50 billion, and both boards approved the agreement unanimously. At closing, two Range representatives, including CEO Dennis L. Degner, join EQT's board. The companies put combined net production above 9 Bcfe a day from nearly 3 million net acres, and they expect base annual synergies of $325 million within two years. The release does not disclose headcounts or plans for the Fort Worth office. Both companies canceled their Oct. 28 earnings calls but will still release third-quarter results after the market close today. Closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027, pending regulatory and shareholder approvals. The companies also see a path to more than $600 million in long-term synergies. If the deal closes as planned, a Fort Worth gas producer will be run from Pennsylvania.

## OPEN WEIGHTS

~ Verdicts, weighed in the open.

**DOWN: OpenAI.** Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton sued OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman on Oct. 23 in Collin County district court, alleging deceptive trade practices in marketing ChatGPT as safe while failing to protect users, including children. The Texas Tribune reports the suit seeks to bar minors from ChatGPT and to require that any new model be approved by a court-appointed evaluator. If that injunction lands, OpenAI's most important beta tester will be picked by a court in Collin County.

> The full story: [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/23/ken-paxton-chatgpt-openai-lawsuit-texas-attorney-general/), [Texas Attorney General](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-sues-openai-and-ceo-sam-altman-lying-users-and-harming-texans)

**UP: Texas Capital Bancshares.** The Dallas bank earned $99.4 million for common stockholders in the third quarter, up from $80.6 million in the second, as revenue reached $356.6 million and net interest income hit a record $275.0 million. CEO Rob C. Holmes called it "a steadily improving, and increasingly diversified earnings profile." Deposits fell $1.2 billion and mortgage finance loans $1.0 billion in the same three months, which makes "increasingly diversified" a generous word for a smaller mortgage book.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

McKinney National Airport, which passengers will book as DTX and pilots will know as TKI, begins commercial service Nov. 11 with Houston-based Avelo Airlines. The first flight goes to Fort Myers, Florida, with 11 destinations in all by December. The four-gate, 46,000-square-foot terminal sits in Collin County, about 30 miles north of downtown Dallas. It cost about $79 million, including a $14.8 million TxDOT grant, and the city says it will not raise the local property tax rate. Planners expect about 200,000 passengers in year one, against roughly 85.7 million a year at DFW. Officials expect the new service to add $400 million to an airport that generates close to $300 million a year. Airport Director Ken Carley says of travelers, "They will save time, money, and hassle by choosing DTX over the other two airports in the region."
