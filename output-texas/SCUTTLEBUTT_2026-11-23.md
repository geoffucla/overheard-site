## THE LEAD

Fermi America co-founder Toby Neugebauer, who was fired in April, alleges in a Dallas County lawsuit that Cody Campbell, chair of the Texas Tech University System Board of Regents, pressed the company for lucrative deals, the Texas Tribune reported Friday. Fermi is building its Project Matador data center campus on about 5,000 acres of Texas Tech land near Amarillo under a 99-year lease. The suit, filed Nov. 9, says a February draft consulting proposal from Double Eagle, the Fort Worth firm Campbell co-founded, sought $150 million in stock options and the right to own at least half of any natural gas pipeline supplying the campus. It also alleges Campbell violated state conflict-of-interest laws. Campbell said, "This is all ridiculous and untrue, but we are going to withhold any comment, due to pending litigation." A Fermi spokesperson called the allegations false and said Double Eagle submitted its proposal at Neugebauer's invitation. Texas law bars state officers from accepting compensation that could impair their independent judgment.

> The full story: [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/20/texas-tech-cody-campbell-board-fermi-data-center-matador/)

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, sweet or unsweetened.

ERCOT finalized a six-year extension for CEO Pablo Vegas on Thursday, worth up to $29.5 million with payouts through 2032. The deal had been on hold for months after the ERCOT board and the Public Utility Commission approved it unanimously in September, alongside a 30% raise for board members that drew criticism from Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, who said he was "disgusted and outraged." PUC Chair Thomas Gleeson said, "Reliability matters most, and Texas must have steady and accountable leadership at ERCOT." ERCOT is funded mainly by a $0.61 per megawatt-hour fee that is typically passed on to customers, and it expects nearly $486 million in revenue this year. Average residential rates are up about 40% since 2020, to 16.11 cents per kWh.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

Dallas-Fort Worth ranked eighth among 250 U.S. metros for patents granted the week of Oct. 27, with 139 tied to North Texas. Texas Instruments led the region with 21. Netflix won a patent for managing live-broadcast playback so a broadcaster can control whether viewers scrub backward, by making segments "unseekable." Christopher Newton of North Richland Hills and Wei Wei of Plano were on the team of six. Stahl Industries of Dallas patented a system that sets a baseline for each AI agent in a group and flags outputs over a threshold, granted in 218 days. NCH Corp. of Irving patented automatic disinfectant dosing for livestock drinking water.
