## THE LEAD

Mavria, a modular data center startup based in The Woodlands north of Houston, announced a $130 million raise this week across pre-seed and seed rounds at a $1 billion post-money valuation. Paradigm led the round, with AMP PBC, Critical Frontier, Dream Ventures and Liquid 2 Ventures also investing. Three former U.S. Department of Energy staffers founded the company over the summer, and CEO Adam Blake leads about 35 employees. Its Terrain product is a factory-built 5-megawatt unit with power distribution, direct liquid cooling, controls and racks. The first unit, built at a Houston-area factory, goes into testing in early 2027, with production ramping by mid-2027. Mavria sells the units and does not operate a cloud or sell compute. Conventional builds take 3 to 6 years and cost an estimated $10 million to $37 million per megawatt. Blake told Bisnow, "Our power infrastructure, manufacturing capacity and supply chains are nowhere near ready to meet the demand from AI."

## THE LEDGER

~ Money in, money out, receipts attached.

Archrock, $450 million, Acquisition. Houston-based Archrock, which provides natural gas compression services, agreed to buy HiVolt Energy Holdings, a Midland, Texas, provider of prime-power microgrids. The price is $415 million in cash at closing plus up to $35 million in earnouts, about 6.1 times HiVolt's midpoint 2028 adjusted EBITDA of $70 million to $75 million. Closing is expected in early 2027, and HiVolt will run as a separate segment under CEO Josh Evans. (Source: [Power Technology](https://www.power-technology.com/news/archrock-hivolt-acquisition/))

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, sweet or unsweetened.

Jon Rosenthal, who won a Railroad Commission seat earlier this month, named a transition team at a Midland news conference on Nov. 13 and set his priorities. The team includes former Republican Railroad Commissioner Barry Smitherman, and sitting commissioner Jim Wright will advise. Rosenthal wants to double the agency's roughly $500 million budget, raise pay for about 1,100 employees, streamline permitting, increase production and plug orphan wells more cheaply. "My goal is to help this commission be more effective and transparent," he said. He added, "I don't think it would be fair of me to lay a bunch of extra work on an already overworked workforce." The Permian delivered nearly half of U.S. oil last year.

## OPEN WEIGHTS

**UP: Colette Kress.** Nvidia's CFO is now interim CEO and keeps the CFO role, after Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO and director effective immediately, an 8-K filed November 12 says. **UP: Brett Giroir.** Dallas-based OncoNano Medicine made its interim CEO permanent, and he already chairs the board he joined in 2021. Giroir, a former acting FDA commissioner and White House coronavirus testing czar, has run the company since May, when he replaced Dr. Kartik Krishnan. Board member John Dyett said he "is the right leader for this next phase." OncoNano has 11 employees in North Texas, and its lead candidate, ONM-501, is in a Phase 1 trial.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

Rex Real Estate's Rex Glendenning expects about 18 months for land prices to level out in Dallas-Fort Worth, he said at Bisnow's Texas Residential Summit at Southfork. Hillwood's residential starts peaked at 60,000 in 2022 and are on pace for about 38,000 this year, with about 35,000 expected next year. Fred Balda of Hillwood said "When you look back at 2019, that's about the same level of activity that we had then, and that was a good year." Glendenning said "We always get out … over our skis a little too far, and we put a little bit too many lots on the ground."
