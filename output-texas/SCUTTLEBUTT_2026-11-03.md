## THE LEAD

Q2 Holdings, the Austin-based provider of digital banking software to banks and credit unions, named its chief financial officer co-CEO effective Oct. 29, as it reported third quarter results. Jonathan Price shares the title with Matt Flake, who remains chairman and president. Price joins the board and stays CFO while the company searches for a successor. Revenue for the quarter ended Sept. 30 was $225.8 million, up 12% from a year earlier. GAAP net income was $33.2 million, compared with $15.0 million. Adjusted EBITDA was $67.8 million, a 30.0% margin. Subscription ARR rose 13.5% to $846.0 million, and backlog was about $2.8 billion. The company raised full-year revenue guidance to $888.0 million to $892.0 million and adjusted EBITDA guidance to $253.0 million to $256.0 million. Price said the results "exceeded the high end of our guidance on both revenue and adjusted EBITDA." The change leaves a bank software vendor with a CFO carrying two jobs during a successor search.

> The full story: [Q2 Holdings release (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001410384/000141038426000062/a260930q2ex9918k.htm)

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, sweet or unsweetened.

Houston-based Alpha Omega Power, a utility-scale battery storage developer owned by Fengate Asset Management, expanded its supply contract with Samsung SDI America, adding 1 gigawatt-hour to its existing agreement. Samsung SDI is making the cells and modules at a plant in Indiana and plans delivery by the end of 2026. The systems are Samsung Battery Box 1.7 units, which offer greater energy density than the prior model. Alpha Omega plans to use them in ERCOT and Western Electricity Coordinating Council markets. Its 200-megawatt Vertus facility in Galveston County came online in October. Energy Capital quotes CEO Paul Choi as saying, "This second agreement strengthens our domestic supply chain supporting the next generation of energy infrastructure."

## OPEN WEIGHTS

~ Verdicts, weighed in the open.

**UP: Advanced Manufacturing Partners.** AMP and the American Center for Manufacturing and Innovation are opening a flagship prototyping factory in Austin, ACMI's first Texas project, which is set to be fully operational this fall. It opens with roughly $30 million in advanced equipment, along with a manufacturing floor, lab space and offices, so defense, aerospace and medical hardware companies can move from prototype toward production without buying their own machines. AMP's Nicholas Bass put it simply, saying, "Our facility is, for them, ready to go."

> The factory floor: [Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/east-austin/business/amp-picks-austin-for-flagship-prototyping-facility-to-boost-advanced-manufacturing/)

**DOWN: Phunware.** The Austin wayfinding-app company used its latest shareholder letter to describe a pivot to an AI "guest intelligence" platform. The company says it "wound down our advertising business" and started raising prices. Its AI Concierge is out of beta at a large Caribbean resort, but CEO Dmitry Kroshka also wrote, "Our fixed cost base remains large relative to revenue, and we continue to operate at a loss." The same letter says "we have ample liquidity and carry no debt," and that stays true only as long as the loss allows it.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

Plano voters decide Proposition A today, and the result is not known as of this writing. The measure would authorize new taxes tied to a $3 billion sports and entertainment district at The Shops at Willow Bend, including a new Dallas Stars arena. Each tax is "up to" a stated rate, among them a 10% admissions tax. Plano has pledged up to $700 million toward the arena, and officials say property tax rates would not change. A final master development agreement still needs approval regardless of the vote. Opposition group Stop the Stars Arena argues some taxes would hit Plano residents. MMR Realty Advisors' Victor F. Russell used the words "significant chaos" for game-day visitors.

> The full story: [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/dallas-ft-worth/mixed-use/plano-voters-to-decide-on-new-taxes-tied-to-dallas-stars-3b-sports-and-entertainment-district)
