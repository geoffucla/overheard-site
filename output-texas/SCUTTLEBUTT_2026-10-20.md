## THE LEAD

Texas environmental regulators kept processing data center permits after Gov. Greg Abbott ordered a pause, according to an analysis released Monday by FracTracker Alliance and Public Citizen. The groups counted 35 actions by the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality on data center projects after Abbott's Sept. 21 letter to the agency's executive director, Kelly Keel. TCEQ fast-tracked or assigned engineers to 14 permits, approved stormwater coverage for 11, certified emissions estimates for seven and issued public notice for three, the groups said. On Sept. 22 it mailed preliminary approval notices for three projects, including VoltaGrid's ABI-1 power plant and Nexus's self-powered data center in Hubbard. An engineer finished an initial review Oct. 9 of a generator application from Edged Energy in Dallas. The directive pauses data center permits until ERCOT completes its review, expected in December, and TCEQ owed Abbott's office a compliance update by Oct. 19. "Our research uncovered glaring inconsistencies between regulatory actions and Governor Abbott's directives," said Sam Law, FracTracker's communications manager.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

Austin-based staffing app Indeed Flex is adding AI to hiring. Applicants can be screened by a person or by AI, which interviews at any hour. A machine learning system offers each shift first to the best-matched workers, judged on skills, past Indeed Flex shifts and prior work for that business. Workers also get a rating that helps companies choose who covers their shifts. "Our machine learning algorithm works out the best workers for that role and offers it to those workers first," CEO Novo Constare said. Sign-up took about 10 minutes for one teacher, TJ Johnson, and requires a Social Security number. The company did not disclose user counts or financials.

> The full story: [CBS Austin](https://cbsaustin.com/news/tech-this-out/need-extra-cash-this-app-finds-you-a-shift-in-ten-minutes)

Dallas-based Colossal Biosciences has unveiled GenoMorph, an AI platform for finding the gene edits that would revive the dodo. The machine has examined the training set with professional courtesy and found no dodo embryos. From it the system flagged about 2,300 DNA elements that may shape the beak, none yet confirmed to shape anything. Anna Keyte, the company's avian species director, has said, "You cannot train AI to answer a precise developmental question using just generic data." The machine notes that this precision rests on hundreds of chicken, quail, duck and pigeon samples, and that the dodo is none of them. Confirmed edits would go into the Nicobar pigeon, the dodo's closest living relative. The company is valued at $10.32 billion. Its Texas colony holds 36 Nicobar pigeons, which comes to roughly $287 million and 64 unconfirmed beak candidates per pigeon. Each pigeon has one beak.
