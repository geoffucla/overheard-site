## THE LEAD

Disruptive Technology Advisers, a venture firm headquartered in Dallas, has closed its Disruptive Flagship Fund at $10 billion, the largest fund in Texas. Disruptive was founded in Los Angeles in 2012, moved to Austin in 2021 and relocated to Dallas in 2024. The fund will invest up to $1 billion each in about 10 growth- or late-stage companies focused on artificial intelligence, AI infrastructure, and defense and national security. Investors include sovereign wealth funds, family offices and institutions, plus a growing investor base in North Texas. Limited partners pushed the firm to create the pooled fund. Disruptive previously raised money deal by deal, and investors wanted consistent access to its investments. Alex Davis, founder and CEO, said, "The best private technology companies are growing larger, staying private longer and requiring more capital than ever before." Portfolio companies, past and current, include Palantir, Stripe, Databricks and Shield AI. The fund gives Dallas the capacity for billion-dollar checks into AI and defense companies.

> Read more: [Dallas Innovates](https://dallasinnovates.com/dallas-based-disruptive-raises-10b-flagship-fund/)

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, sweet or unsweetened.

ServerDome Global says it plans a $15 billion first phase for a data center campus at Hueco Ranch in Hudspeth County, covering 700 acres with 10 dome-shaped buildings. The company says the site would run on its own one-gigawatt natural gas power plant that would not connect to the electric grid. Managing director and co-founder Ryan Wallace said, "There will be no interconnection agreement whatsoever." ServerDome says a closed cooling loop would limit water use, though it gave no volume figure. It says the campus would employ 200 to 300 people once fully operational. CEO David Keegan said the remote location was chosen partly because data centers can generate noise. Residents of Desert Haven, several miles away, asked for answers on noise, water and power at a community meeting Monday. No county officials were quoted.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

Micro Center opens its first Central Texas store at 12707 N Mopac Expy in Austin on Nov. 13, with a Grand Opening Celebration that Friday. It is the chain's 31st US store and third in Texas, after Dallas and Houston, and it will employ more than 100 people. The building is the former Fry's Electronics site, which Hoodline reported the chain took over in December 2025. The store stocks more than 20,000 products, including Blackwell-based GPUs for AI development and Ubiquiti UniFi networking gear, and has a 3D printer department with over 500 filament products and a Knowledge Bar for help with builds and repairs. The first customers in line get swag bags, and online sign-ups earn a coupon for a free 128GB flash drive.

> Doors open: [Micro Center via PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/micro-center-arrives-in-austin-texas-third-store-opens-november-13-302919377.html)
