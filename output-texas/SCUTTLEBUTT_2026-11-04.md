## THE LEAD

The Associated Press projected Tuesday that Democrat Gina Hinojosa, an Austin state representative, won the Texas governor's race over Gov. Greg Abbott, putting the state's data center industry under an incoming governor who campaigned on pausing construction. Abbott conceded by phone at about 10 p.m. Central, and results are unofficial with counts incomplete. Hinojosa wants new projects halted until lawmakers pass resident protections, and she would end subsidies. Jefferies estimated her moratorium could put 13.3 gigawatts of announced Texas projects at risk. Qualifying facilities can avoid the 6.25% state sales tax on equipment for up to 20 years, Fast Company reported. A governor can direct agencies only within existing law, and a statewide moratorium could face legal obstacles. Permanent changes to tax exemptions would fall to the Legislature, where Republicans are expected to hold the Senate and control of the House was undecided overnight.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

League City voters have rejected their Houston-area suburb's use of Flock license-plate cameras. In a nonbinding referendum Tuesday, Proposition A asked whether police could keep using the city's 55 cameras on public roads, and just under 54% of about 46,400 ballots favored removal, per the Galveston County elections site. The City Council had pledged in mid-October to remove the cameras if voters said no. The cameras record every passing vehicle, including plate number, make, model, paint color and visible features such as dents and bumper stickers. Texas had roughly 13,000 Flock cameras as of late August, a count that has fallen as cities and counties ended contracts. The cameras come down or get covered within 72 hours of certification, and Kyle and Laredo hold nonbinding votes next May.

> Read more: [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/03/texas-flock-cameras-league-city-results-2026-elections/)

## CORRECTIONS AND UPDATES

~ Where we were wrong, or the story moved.

On Tuesday we reported that Plano voters were deciding Proposition A, the venue taxes tied to a new Dallas Stars arena. Early returns have Prop A trailing, with about 54% against and 46% for, and the count is not yet canvassed. The final count was not available to us as of writing.
