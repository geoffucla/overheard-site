## THE LEAD

Austin-based Bumble agreed Oct. 13 to be taken private by Ithaca Holdings in an all-cash deal valuing the dating app company at about $1.3 billion, and its headquarters are expected to remain in Austin. Ithaca will pay $6.05 per Class A share, about 138% over the unaffected closing price on Oct. 9. An independent special committee chaired by Ann Mather recommended the deal, and the board approved it unanimously. Founder and CEO Whitney Wolfe Herd recused herself from deliberations and the vote. Affiliates of Blackstone and Wolfe Herd approved the deal by written consent, so no shareholder vote is needed. Closing awaits regulatory approvals, and no date was disclosed. Ithaca co-CEOs Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman will become Bumble's co-CEOs after closing, working with Wolfe Herd. Mather said "this transaction with Ithaca represents the best outcome for our shareholders." The premium still leaves Bumble at under a tenth of the $14 billion valuation Bumble carried at its 2021 Nasdaq listing.

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, steeped plainly.

Riverway Group, a Houston welding and fabrication firm, and Offshore Data Center, LLC announced a partnership Oct. 14 to build gas-powered offshore data center platforms on the U.S. Gulf Coast. Offshore Data Center develops platforms that sit next to a gas source, with concepts up to 1 gigawatt for AI training and smaller configurations for edge and inference computing. CEO Art J. Schroeder, Jr. said offshore gas often goes undeveloped because "getting it to market doesn't pencil out," and that "Riverway gives us a way to build these platforms here in the United States." Data Center Dynamics reported that the announcement gives no timelines, capacity or cost figures and names no customers, and that the claims come from the company.
