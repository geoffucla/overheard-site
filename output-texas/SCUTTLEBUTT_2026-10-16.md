## THE LEAD

Gov. Greg Abbott ordered the Texas Water Development Board on Sept. 14 to penalize data centers that do not report their water use, and Attorney General Ken Paxton opened an investigation. State law sets the maximum penalty at a $500 fine for a Class C misdemeanor, and officials do not know whether any has been issued. The board says it cannot prosecute and was told to notify county and district attorneys. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality says the law does not allow it to revoke or suspend permits already issued. Data centers are generally not eligible for the state funding officials threatened to withhold. Of 334 facilities identified, 233 never submitted the survey. In 2025, 97 facilities reported a combined 1.4 billion gallons, and nearly 30% of the 329 surveyed responded. Raising the fines would require legislation, and the 2027 session begins in January. Meta says its Fort Worth facility averages 72 million gallons a year. For operators, the cost of not filing stays at $500 until lawmakers act.

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, steeped plainly.

The Taylor City Council unanimously approved a data center campus of up to 1GW, formerly called Project Mustang, on 664 acres in Williamson County, about 35 miles northeast of Austin. Developers PowerHouse Data Centers and Big Watt Digital will power it with a new 750MW natural gas plant behind the meter, meaning dedicated on-site generation. First power is targeted for the first half of 2028, starting with a 500MW phase. Big Watt co-CEO Alex Hern said the project addresses "access to reliable power" and avoids "relying solely on incremental grid capacity." That matters because Gov. Abbott's pause on new data center interconnection approvals exempts projects that bring their own power. PowerHouse also has a 201MW data center in Irving that topped out in March.

## UNSUITABLE FOR GENERAL RELEASE

~ Not cleared for the weekday edition.

I spent the week reading crackdowns, and the announcement and the excuse now come stapled together. As reported above, the order against data centers that skip the water survey rests on a $500 maximum fine that officials cannot say was ever issued, and the water board, unable to prosecute, was told to send the matter to local prosecutors. That makes the enforcement arm of the order a forwarding address.

Every follow-up found another locked door. The environmental agency cannot pull permits already issued, and the threatened state money is money data centers generally do not get. Meanwhile 233 of 334 facilities skipped the survey, and fining each the maximum comes to $116,500, with the water board's part in collecting it ending at the stamp.

The grid pause runs on the same design, with better engineering. More than 1,800 projects are waiting to connect, about 90% of them data centers, and any project that brings its own power is exempt. The Taylor council unanimously approved a 1GW campus with its own 750MW gas plant. That is a lot of power plant to build in order to comply with a pause.

San Antonio is at least arguing about distance. The mayor's plan includes a "1,000-foot setback from the perimeter," one staff option uses 500 feet, and it was unclear whether the two stack. Councilwoman Misty Spears warned, "If we put too many restrictions here, they're not even going to look at us."

So the board cannot prosecute, the agency cannot pull a permit, the pause opens for anyone carrying a gas plant, and the city is still doing arithmetic. The water board does have its orders to hand the matter to local prosecutors, and if it sends them a survey, nearly 30% should write back.
