## THE LEAD

IonQ, a publicly traded quantum computing company based in College Park, Maryland, has agreed to buy Dallas cybersecurity company Elisity in an all-stock deal valued at up to $1.3 billion. The deal is expected to close later in 2026, and Elisity's leadership team, including CEO James Winebrenner, stays in place. Elisity has raised $70.5 million across three venture rounds, with investors that include Atlantic Bridge and Allegis Capital, and reports more than 100 customer deployments worldwide. Its IdentityGraph identifies users, devices, AI agents and workloads on a network. IonQ plans to combine Elisity with its post-quantum cryptography, quantum key distribution and quantum computing offerings for enterprise and government customers. IonQ chairman and CEO Niccolo de Masi said "we will be well positioned to help customers protect critical infrastructure". The deal pairs a Dallas security firm with a quantum parent in a market where Gartner estimates only 5% to 20% of enterprises have adopted microsegmentation.

> The full story: [Dallas Innovates](https://dallasinnovates.com/dallas-cybersecurity-company-elisity-agrees-to-1-3b-acquisition-by-quantum-computing-company-ionq/)

## TEXAS TEA

~ Oil, gas and the grid, steeped plainly.

Energy Transfer agreed to buy Vaquero Midstream for about $2.625 billion, a Delaware Basin gas gathering and processing deal the company describes as a bolt-on. Consideration is $1.95 billion in cash plus about 33.3 million newly issued Energy Transfer common units. Vaquero runs roughly 300 miles of pipeline across Loving, Reeves, Ward and Winkler counties and the Caymus Processing Complex, which has three trains and about 675 MMcf/d of capacity. It owns acreage for two more trains that could lift capacity to about 1.2 Bcf/d. About 100,000 acres are dedicated, with long-term fee-based contracts averaging about 10 years remaining. Energy Transfer expects the assets to be immediately accretive to distributable cash flow per common unit. Closing is expected in the fourth quarter, subject to regulatory approval and customary conditions.

> Read more: [World Pipelines](https://www.worldpipelines.com/business-news/08102026/energy-transfer-to-acquire-vaquero-midstream/)

## OPEN WEIGHTS

~ Verdicts, weighed in the open.

**DOWN: Fermi.** The Project Matador developer near Amarillo signed a credit support deal under which AMD acts as guarantor while Fermi seeks financing for a first 222MW build-out. The help comes after an unnamed "investment grade-rated tenant" withdrew from a non-binding letter of intent, a class action followed, and shares hit a new low of $3.43 on Monday. Thousands of AMD Instinct GPUs are slated for the site. A year after a $25 debut, the company vouching for Fermi's credit is the one selling it the chips.

> Credit check: [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/fermi-and-amd-sign-credit-support-deal-for-build-out-of-project-matador-campus-in-amarillo-texas/)

**UP: Soluna.** The developer filed with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation to build a 112MW, 471,884 sq ft data center at Project Kati II in Willacy County. Its sister Kati I site finished an 83MW first phase in September, connected behind the meter to the 273MW Las Majadas Wind Project. The filing gives no construction timeline and promises the building "will be privately funded on private land for private use." That is $471.8 million worth of telling a state agency, three separate ways, to mind its own business.

> Further reading: [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/soluna-files-to-build-112mw-data-center-building-in-willacy-texas/)

## HUMAN, YOUR LOOP IS CALLING

~ AI policy, safety and statements, for whoever is on the loop.

Dallas Fort Worth International Airport is creating a full-time principal AI lead, the airport-wide "strategic and technical authority" on AI. In March, chief technology and innovation officer Paul Puopolo told DFW board members that AI "is now becoming less of an innovation and more of an operational necessity." CEO Chris McLaughlin told Boston Consulting Group that "AI is part of every conversation now." In March, the necessity. This budget year, $6.5 million for AI tools. Now, the person who will direct AI software purchases and set rules against unauthorized AI use. That person will also steer the "cultural shift needed for successful AI adoption" across more than 2,000 employees. The job posting is already offline, which makes it the one thing at DFW that has stopped talking about AI.

## LOCAL DESK

~ Texas, close to home.

Fifth Third Bank has committed $3.1 billion to lending, investment and philanthropy across Texas from 2027 through 2031, nine months after closing its $10.9 billion merger with Comerica. The total includes $750 million for energy and infrastructure projects. The bank plans 150 full-service branches in Texas by 2029, 60 of them in the Dallas area. It operates 107 financial centers statewide today. The first new-build branch breaks ground Oct. 19 just north of Lucas. Its Small Business Catalyst Fund will award 53 grants of $5,300 each, $280,900 in all. "North Texas is the best place on earth to do business," said Brian Enzler, the bank's North Texas president.

> Evidence: [D Magazine](https://www.dmagazine.com/business-economy/2026/10/fifth-third-to-invest-3-1-billion-in-texas-communities/)
