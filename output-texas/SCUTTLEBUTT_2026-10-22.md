## THE LEAD

Texas Instruments, the Dallas-based chipmaker, reported third-quarter revenue of $6.06 billion on Oct. 21, up 28% from a year earlier. Net income rose 78% to $2.43 billion. Earnings per share rose 77% to $2.62. CEO Haviv Ilan said, "Revenue increased 11% sequentially and 28% from the same quarter a year ago." Growth came across industrial, automotive and data center markets. Cash flow from operations was $3.42 billion in the quarter and $9.90 billion over the trailing 12 months. Free cash flow for that period was $8.42 billion, up 249% and equal to 40.6% of revenue. Over the trailing 12 months, TI paid $5.17 billion in dividends, up 5%, cut buybacks 61% to $636 million and received $1.10 billion in CHIPS Act incentives. For the fourth quarter, TI expects revenue of $5.80 billion to $6.30 billion and EPS of $2.45 to $2.79. The figures give a current measure of demand across three major chip markets.

## AUTOMATIC REPLIES

~ The machine responds to the day's most unreasonable announcement.

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality has temporarily disabled STEERS, its electronic filing system "used for automatic authorizations," after watchdogs said it had issued stormwater permits for data center construction, a month after, as reported Tuesday, the governor ordered a halt to "all permits related to data center projects." The agency suspended the permits the watchdogs named, then the portal itself, which also handles some air and water pollution permit applications. A company seeking a stormwater permit files a "notice of intent," which the system approves on arrival, so Texas built a machine that says yes and only now learned it had no other setting. Public Citizen's Kathryn Guerra said the agency's rubber stamp "worked so well, even the agency had difficulty stopping it." Applications, says spokesman Ryan Vise, "must be physically provided to TCEQ." Texas has solved automatic approval by requiring that each application arrive on paper, which at last makes the paperwork as heavy as the project.
