## THE LEAD

Anthropic, the San Francisco-based AI company, priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range it had set, Bloomberg, Reuters and the Financial Times reported late Wednesday. Shares are expected to begin trading Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The offering would raise more than $68 billion if all shares sell and the price holds, and more than $94 billion once private placements are included. SiliconANGLE reported that market capitalization could come in above the valuation implied at the range. A revised prospectus shows revenue of $33.2 billion for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, and cautions, "We do not expect our revenue will continue to grow at comparable rates." Anthropic co-founder and CEO Dario Amodei said, "If we do this right, we can create an age of miracles." Analyst Dan Ives said, "It's a white-knuckle period and it's a vote of confidence."

## OPEN WEIGHTS

**UP: Palo Alto Networks.** Palo Alto Networks beat forecasts on revenue and earnings in its fiscal first quarter, which ended Oct. 31. Revenue rose 39% to $3.44 billion against $3.31 billion expected, and adjusted earnings of $1.05 a share topped the $0.97 estimate. > The numbers: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/18/palo-alto-networks-bets-4-5b-on-ai-startup-applied-compute-as-earnings-top-forecasts/)

**DOWN: Microsoft.** Its official agent-framework Python package on PyPI was hijacked, and The Register reports evidence points to compromised administrator credentials in Microsoft's GitHub organization. After malicious version 1.26.1 was reverted, the attacker uploaded 1.26.2 through Microsoft's own official publisher account. The malware runs without the package ever being imported and harvests environment variables and secrets for Azure, Git, Docker and Hugging Face. It also installs the genuine 1.26.0 so the package keeps working.

> Rotate your keys, then read this: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/18/microsofts-official-python-agent-framework-hijacked-to-distribute-malware/5310858)

## LOCAL DESK

~ The Bay Area, close to home.

Mayor Daniel Lurie announced Nov. 18 that San Francisco's Planning Department, Department of Building Inspection and Permit Center will merge into a new Department of Planning and Building. Because the departments were written into the city charter, voters had to approve the change, and they did so in November with 60%. Sarah Dennis Phillips, head of Planning, will lead the new department, and DBI's interim director, David Kane, becomes Building Official reporting to her. In January the mayor's office said the merger should proceed without layoffs. Lurie said the department has "one clear mission: Make it faster and easier to build in San Francisco." As of March the city averaged 280 days to approve a building permit, among the longest in the country.
