## THE LEAD

Meta and OpenAI each announced Wednesday that they are buying a startup that builds tools for AI coding agents. Meta, based in Menlo Park, is acquiring Conductor, whose Mac app runs several agents at once, including Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. OpenAI, based in San Francisco, is acquiring developer tooling startup Pierre Computer. Prices were not disclosed. Conductor, which says more than 100,000 people use it, has halted new sign-ups and made paid features free for Pro subscribers. Its cloud service becomes read-only Nov. 13 and shuts down Nov. 20, after which user data there is deleted. People familiar with the deal told Axios that Meta expects the team's speed at shipping to help build its next generation of AI coding tools. OpenAI plans to add Pierre's paid hosting features to Codex and will keep offering Trees and Diffs under an open-source license. Observers saw the deal as "a shot at Microsoft Corp.'s open-source software repository GitHub." For developers who use Conductor's cloud, the deadline to move their work is Nov. 20.

## THE LEDGER

~ Money in, money out, receipts attached.

Guava, $12.5 million, Seed. The Los Angeles company builds a developer platform for AI voice agents that handle customer calls such as scheduling appointments and live support, running its own speech recognition, language models and speech synthesis. Acrew Capital led the round, announced this week, with Flourish Ventures, WillowTree Ventures, Clocktower Ventures and Commerce Ventures. Co-founder and CEO Omri Dahan said, "It sounds fine in a demo but breaks down on real, high-stakes calls." ## LOCAL DESK

~ The Bay Area, close to home.

The Phelan Building at 780 Market St. received a notice of default at the end of October for missed payments on a $215 million loan. AIG holds the loan, with $192 million still owed, and per tax records is seeking to foreclose. The owner, Phelan Building LLC, is linked to Patrick and Ellen Hotung, who control Buffalo-based Mainliberty and bought the property from Thor Equities in 2016 for $374 million. An auction would be the next stage. The largest retail space has sat empty since Marshalls left in 2021, though Figma has leased several floors since spring 2024 and a Verizon store and a Little Sweet boba tea outpost remain. The 1908 landmark replaced a building that burned after the 1906 earthquake.
