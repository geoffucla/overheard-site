# Overheard in the Bay

Designed for human consumption by Geoff Allen

## THE ONE THING

Etched, the Silicon Valley inference-chip startup, is reportedly fielding offers at a $40 billion to $50 billion valuation. In September it was worth $21 billion, which means a company founded by three Harvard dropouts has roughly doubled in the time it takes most of us to renew a gym membership and then ignore it. The stated case is a claim that its chips process more tokens, faster and cheaper, than Nvidia's, backed by $1 billion in customer orders as of July. My read is that the product may well be real and the price is still a different matter. A valuation that doubles in a month is the market telling you it has run out of ways to say it is excited, and it has now moved on to arithmetic as a form of enthusiasm. Every inference-chip founder in the Valley will have seen the number by lunch, and several will have quietly updated their own decks.

## WHO'S UP / WHO'S DOWN

**UP: Factory.** An update on the story from last Thursday's edition, in which the company's CEO accused his own board adviser of leaking to Cognition. Menlo Ventures has now announced a significant investment, days after Vinod Khosla publicly called Factory a "struggling second tier competitor." Being insulted by an investor in your rival, and then paid by another investor to prove him wrong, is a remarkably efficient way to run a fundraising process. The $5 billion mark now has a second set of fingerprints on it.

> The full story: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/after-factorys-public-spat-with-khosla-menlo-proudly-invests/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maryroeloffs/2026/10/01/vinod-khosla-openly-trashes-his-own-ai-portfolio-startup-as-struggling-second-tier-competitor/)

**UP: FieldAI.** The robot-software company has reportedly raised $700 million at a $10 billion valuation, more than four times its mark of just over a year ago. It builds the brains rather than the bodies, which is the correct side of the robotics business to be on if you would prefer not to discover how many things can go wrong with a hydraulic knee. Physical AI is the one corner of the market where nobody is asking for a demo, only for a larger number.

> Further reading: [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/10/62150844/fieldai-700-million-10-billion-valuation-physical-ai)

**DOWN: Vinod Khosla's sense of occasion.** He is an investor in both Factory and Cognition, and chose to describe one of them in public as second tier while the other was being accused of poaching its adviser. There are fine reasons to hold a view on two portfolio companies. Broadcasting it, in a week when one of them has just raised money from a competitor, is a bold interpretation of the fiduciary relationship.

> For the skeptical: [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maryroeloffs/2026/10/01/vinod-khosla-openly-trashes-his-own-ai-portfolio-startup-as-struggling-second-tier-competitor/)

## THE SCUTTLEBUTT

Returning to Instinct, last covered in the October 2 edition. The agent with its own phone number now works in group chats, including for friends who have no account, which is a polite way of saying it has begun to attend conversations it was not originally invited to. The company says personal agents must ask permission before connecting to a group agent and that the group version is siloed from your private data. Founder Noah Shinn is the one making that promise. Notice what is still missing from the coverage, namely any user count, for a company valued at $10 billion on the strength of an invite-only beta. The privacy design is carefully reassuring. The adoption data remains in a drawer somewhere.

> Earlier coverage: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/instinct-brings-its-ai-agent-to-group-chats-even-for-friends-without-an-account/), [Earlier edition](https://bay.overheardnews.com/editions/2026-10-02/)

The detail in the Etched story that deserves a second look is the investor list. Jane Street led the most recent round, and Jane Street is also a customer. That is a perfectly normal arrangement in the sense that it now happens weekly, and a slightly circular one in the sense that the customer's check is a component of the customer's order backlog's credibility. None of this is improper. It does mean the $1 billion in orders deserves to be read with the same interest one brings to a restaurant review written by the owner's brother-in-law.

> Evidence: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/etched-fields-funding-offers-at-40b-valuation-sources-say/)

## BAY AREA TEXTURE

SF Tech Week runs through Sunday, presented for the fourth consecutive San Francisco year by Andreessen Horowitz, and it is deliberately scattered across founder houses, hackathons and receptions rather than parked in a convention hall. The events are free, in the sense that the price is zero and admission is not guaranteed, so a good portion of the week consists of applying to be allowed into a room to meet people who also applied. Disrupt follows at Moscone West on October 13. The Valley's dinner calendar is now so full that it may soon be easier to be a founder than a guest.

## A STRONGLY WORDED COMPLAINT

I should like to be perfectly clear about Ghost. A 19-year-old founder, Zain Javaid, has raised $11 million from Andreessen Horowitz for a computer that costs $3,499. Very well. It has no screen. You operate it from your phone, or by speaking to it. Let me understand this. You are selling me a computer that I cannot look at, and I am to control it using a second computer, which I already own, and which does have a screen. Sir, that is not a product, that is a phone with an expensive accomplice. It contains a serious Nvidia graphics card, I am assured, and no one, including the company, can read my data, which is also what I was told about my last filing cabinet. Preorders open tomorrow. I shall be watching the queue with great interest.

> Should you wish to verify: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/at-19-ghost-founder-raises-11-million-to-build-a-3499-computer-for-your-personal-ai/)

## THE CONTRARIAN READ

Reflection AI released Beam, a 501-billion-parameter open-weight model, and the press has largely framed it as America's answer to the Chinese open models. That is the company's own framing, and the performance claim underneath it, parity with Z.ai's GLM-5.2 at three to four times less inference compute, is also the company's own, and unverified. The honest read is that a $25 billion valuation, $4.7 billion raised and more than $7 billion in compute commitments buys you the right to match a rival who is already out. Matching a free competitor is a business only if enterprises are willing to pay a premium for the flag on the box. That may well be true. It deserves to be tested by customers rather than assumed by headline writers.

> Read more: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/reflection-debuts-beam-a-open-weight-ai-model-to-rival-chinese-models-at-lower-compute-cost/)

## THREE THINGS TO BRING UP TODAY

1. Etched is reportedly worth $21 billion in September and $40 billion or more today, so ask what exactly happened to the product in the interim.
2. Khosla called Factory second tier and Menlo invested the same week, which makes it the most expensive rebuttal in recent venture history.
3. Ghost is a $3,499 computer with no screen that you run from your phone, and the only fair question is whether the phone gets a commission.
