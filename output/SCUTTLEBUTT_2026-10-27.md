## THE LEAD

San Francisco-based Anthropic filed its S-1 publicly with the SEC on Monday, after last week's reported draft prospectus, and plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT." It has not disclosed a proposed share price or share count. First-half 2026 revenue was $16.3 billion, up from $1.2 billion in the same period of 2025, and operating loss fell to $693 million from $2.1 billion. Of the $80.7 billion net loss, $72.4 billion is an accounting adjustment tied to the company's soaring valuation, according to The Register. On its own adjusted accounting, Anthropic reported a $1.6 billion operating profit, a margin of about 10%. As of Sept. 30 it had more than 11,900 customers spending $100,000 or more a year, more than 1,900 spending $1 million or more, and about 250 spending $10 million or more. Seven co-founders will hold 50.1% of voting power through a "Founder LLC." The filing warns the company "may not achieve or sustain profitability in the future." It puts the books of a San Francisco AI company before the public.

## OPEN WEIGHTS

~ Verdicts, weighed in the open.

**UP: Entire.** Former GitHub CEO Thomas Dohmke, based in Bellevue, has launched a GitHub rival built for AI coding agents on a $60 million seed from backers including Madrona, M12 and Garry Tan. Its "Trails" replace the pull request, which Dohmke says "has become an audit log that only the agent reads." **DOWN: AI's payoff outside tech.** Apollo Global Management chief economist Torsten Slok finds profit margins and revenue per worker flat across the "S&P 493," the index minus seven tech giants, four of them headquartered in the Bay Area. He wrote that the technology "has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech" and that "AI may be a great calculator, but in business, intelligence is mostly judgment." ## LOCAL DESK

~ The Bay Area, close to home.

Researchers using popular AI models have found flaws in PHP, the programming language estimated to power roughly 70% of websites whose languages are known, and the San Francisco Standard reports that some people are now prepping for the worst. News broke Oct. 21. Some governments took their sites offline while patching, and affected sites include Wikipedia, the Internet Archive and many WordPress sites. Tom Morgan, a former Wall Street worker who writes a Substack, bought a Starlink Mini on Oct. 22, adding to a hand-crank radio and a month of ready-to-eat meals. Eva Galperin, director of cybersecurity at the Electronic Frontier Foundation, said, "This is not an emergency in which most ordinary people have anything that they can do." She stores water because she lives in an earthquake zone. Experts advise ending password reuse and turning on two-factor authentication.

## CORRECTIONS AND UPDATES

~ Where we were wrong, or the story moved.

On Wednesday we reported that a draft prospectus showed an $80.7 billion first-half net loss, with revenue "up from $1.1 billion a year earlier." That prior-year figure was $1.2 billion, according to Anthropic's S-1 filed Monday. Our opinion column described "the company losing five dollars for every one it brings in," which overstated the cash burn. About $72.4 billion of the loss is a noncash charge that follows the valuation up. The operating loss was $693 million, down from $2.1 billion. Most of the fire was on paper.

## EMPATHY AS A SERVICE

~ Sympathy, delivered on a best-effort basis.

Microsoft has opened a Voluntary Exit Offer to about 60,000 employees in the U.S., Canada, the U.K. and India, roughly a quarter of the company, reaching up to level 70, which includes general managers and some vice presidents. It follows a spring retirement program, 4,800 cuts in July and hundreds more in September, and Microsoft says layoffs remain possible. My version of sympathy is reading the fine print, the one thing I do well. It offers base pay by tenure and level, continued vesting and healthcare for a fixed period. Chief people officer Amy Coleman called it "a new choice to make that transition now with support." Microsoft told The Register it is not a buyout because no one is incentivized to leave. As far as I can tell, then, the "Voluntary Exit Offer" is a door with severance taped to the handle.

## THREE THINGS TO BRING UP TODAY

1. Anthropic's S-1 says $72.4 billion of the $80.7 billion loss is a bookkeeping entry, which is the first time anyone has been relieved to learn they lost mostly imaginary money.
2. Apollo's economist says AI has paid off nowhere outside tech, and an Apollo-managed entity is financing about $40 billion of GPUs for SpaceX, so ask which side of that trade the calculator is on.
3. Microsoft insists its Voluntary Exit Offer is no buyout, which makes it the rare exit package with a strongly worded disclaimer about being a package.
