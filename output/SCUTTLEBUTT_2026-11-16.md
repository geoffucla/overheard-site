## THE LEAD

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Sunday that Nvidia needs a "dramatic shake-up" and should look outside the company for its next chief executive, according to reporting on his remarks. The Santa Clara chipmaker also faces his demand for answers on whether it knowingly allowed advanced AI chips to reach China in breach of export rules. Separately, a petition to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO has passed 173,000 signatures, with Elon Musk among its backers, though The Register says the petition's authenticity is unverified. Huang resigned Thursday, leaving CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO, and Nvidia reports quarterly earnings Tuesday, the first chance for investors to put these questions to management. Nvidia is also a heavy investor, having joined 105 funding rounds this year according to PitchBook, and investors expect Kress to write fewer large checks and smaller follow-ons to companies it already backs.

## OPEN WEIGHTS

~ Verdicts, weighed in the open.

**DOWN: Tesla's Cybercab pre-orders.** Tesla opened Cybercab pre-orders to select customers for personal use at $29,990, with estimated delivery in December. Owners would pay $299 a month to operate the car, buyers must take delivery in one of a handful of Texas cities, and the vehicle will run only inside geofenced areas. Elon Musk called the monthly fee "too high" and suggested about $115 a month. The car arrives as Tesla plans to stop making the Cybertruck in 2027 and give that factory capacity to more Cybercabs.

> Pre-order details: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/tesla-opens-cybercab-pre-orders-for-personal-use-with-geo-restrictions-and-a-299-monthly-fee/)

**UP: Apple's Health AI Study.** Apple released a US research app on Sunday, built with healthcare AI company OpenEvidence, that answers health questions from clinical sources including NEJM, JAMA and Nature. It aims to enroll up to 200,000 owners of an iPhone 15 or later for more than a year, and Apple says shared HealthKit data is anonymized and not used to train models, and that OpenEvidence deletes query and response data after 24 hours. Participants rate the answers, which means NEJM, JAMA and Nature are now being peer reviewed by iPhone owners.

> Read more: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/)

## CORRECTIONS AND UPDATES

~ Where we were wrong, or the story moved.

On Friday we reported the Microsoft 365 and Exchange Online outage that cost San Francisco city government its Outlook and Teams, without a confirmed cause. Microsoft's preliminary report now traces it to a change prepared in July for its network gateway servers, which carried a latent defect that exhausted CPU and memory on affected machines. Microsoft said "Our load testing tended to focus on weaker hardware with fewer cores." A final report is due Nov. 20. The bug, it turns out, saved its worst for the good computers.

> The post-mortem: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/saas/2026/11/16/microsoft-blames-monster-email-outage-on-software-defect-introduced-in-july-fix/5310026), [Earlier edition](https://bay.termsundisclosed.com/editions/2026-11-13/)
