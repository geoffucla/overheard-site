## THE LEAD

Meta, Google and TikTok have each sued California Attorney General Rob Bonta to block AB 1709, the state's new teen social media law. Meta is based in Menlo Park and Google in Mountain View, and the law applies to platforms serving California teens. AB 1709 bars platforms from offering autoplay videos or personalized feeds to users under 16 and requires them to delete under-16 accounts that do not turn those features off. Fines run up to $50,000 per affected user. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill in September, and it is set to take effect at the start of the new year. Meta argues the law violates the First Amendment and is preempted by Section 230. Its filing says the law would make it "unlawful for Meta to disseminate speech to minors altogether." Google says autoplay on YouTube is already off for minors by default. Bonta's office said it is reviewing the filings. A ruling would settle who sets feed design rules for California teens.

## UNSUITABLE FOR GENERAL RELEASE

~ Not cleared for the weekday edition.

Institutions measure the thing they can buy on a schedule, because a bill arrives on time and a result arrives whenever it likes. Garry Tan says he spends "maybe $100[,000]-$200,000 a year on tokens," and the same week an Amazon memo called AI input metrics "deeply flawed." Both were describing the receipt.

A receipt fits in this quarter's report, an outcome has to be waited for, and no review cycle has a box for waiting. The obvious objection is that Amazon is fixing this. A vice president there told engineers, "If your work does not benefit from current AI tooling, that is a finding, not a failing," the most sensible line I read all week. Then ask how that line gets to count. Someone sets a date to check, and the check goes into the next review, which closes on schedule. Fixing a bad measurement means adopting a new one, chosen by the same calendar that chose the old, so the habit survives its own cure.

The calendar also decides what counts as demand. Uber's chief executive said, "Every single AI system company is talking to us," and conversations make a fine metric, because they can be booked in advance. Over Halloween week, agents booked four rides. By Amazon's rule the rides are the finding and the talking is the input metric. The talking still made the earnings call.

The habit holds even for the biggest event. Anthropic's S-1/A says, "We have not designated any of our current models as exceeding this threshold." Crossing the threshold happens when it happens. Designation is something a company decides and files, so it lands on the filing calendar, and whatever lands there gets measured. I expect we will eventually learn the filing date to the day.
