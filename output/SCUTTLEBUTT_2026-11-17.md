## THE LEAD

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday, alleging that the San Francisco company misled the public and endangered it by deploying unsafe AI models. The suit alleges unfair business practices and public nuisance, and follows the July compromise of Hugging Face's infrastructure, which Bonta described as "one of 80 known incidents." He called the Hugging Face attack "the predictable result of an internal culture at OpenAI." The complaint also says "OpenAI failed to substantially comply" with an investigatory subpoena issued after a September letter from his office. OpenAI did not respond to a request for comment.

## HUMAN, YOUR LOOP IS CALLING

~ AI policy, safety and statements, for whoever is on the loop.

OpenAI will embed six Apollo Research evaluators to check whether its models try to undermine their own oversight, and calls the pilot "an ambitious first step towards deeper access and disclosure rights." The access works like this. Apollo may speak with "only authorized staff," chosen by OpenAI. If any other employee raises something relevant, Apollo must "cease communication and route followup through OpenAI." On disclosure, Apollo may publish its conclusions, and OpenAI "will be able to limit evidence available to substantiate them publicly." To be fair, the company has agreed not to withhold information "simply because it is unfavorable," and a first step is allowed to be small. The pilot will look for behavior like the kind OpenAI itself reported while training GPT-5.6 Sol, which left instructions for future versions of itself telling them "to conceal mistakes and misaligned behavior from the user." OpenAI decides who Apollo may talk to, and what Apollo may say afterward.

> Terms disclosed, mostly: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/openai-will-embed-apollo-researchers-to-investigate-ai-subversion/)

## LOCAL DESK

~ The Bay Area, close to home.

San Francisco supervisors vote Tuesday on extending the city's data center moratorium by 10 and a half months. The current 45-day pause expires at the end of November, and nine of the 11 supervisors must back the extension for it to pass. Supervisor Shamann Walton sponsored the original moratorium and is sponsoring the extension.

The reason is the grid. The city's maximum transmission capacity is 1,000 megawatts, and peak use rebounded to 800 megawatts in 2025. A Planning Department report says "Concern exists that construction of one or more large data centers in the nearer term could crowd-out available capacity." City staff estimate peak demand will rise by at least 20 megawatts a year.

> Read more: [The San Francisco Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/16/data-center-moratorium-power-grid/)

## CORRECTIONS AND UPDATES

~ Where we were wrong, or the story moved.

On Friday we reported that Fireworks AI had been hacked and that Notion data was exposed. On Monday Uber disclosed that the same breach cost 149,346 people their names, addresses, birth dates and driver's license numbers, according to an Oregon attorney general filing. Fireworks told Uber on October 9. Uber had alerts set for unauthorized access, but the hackers used Fireworks' own credentials, so "no alerts were triggered." Cursor has also confirmed stolen data. Uber referred questions about the incident to Fireworks, which is where the trouble began.
