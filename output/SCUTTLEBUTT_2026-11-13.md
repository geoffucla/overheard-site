## THE LEAD

Nvidia said Thursday that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive and a member of its board, effective immediately. The chipmaker, which is headquartered in Santa Clara, announced the change in a press release and an 8-K filing. The announcement gave no reason or compensation terms. Colette Kress, executive vice president and chief financial officer since September 2013, becomes interim CEO and keeps her CFO role. She has worked as a finance executive for nearly 25 years. Harvey C. Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair. The board is searching internal and external candidates for the permanent job. Jones said, "The Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team." Kress said, "NVIDIA's strength has always come from its people." Nvidia reports third-quarter fiscal 2027 results Tuesday, Nov. 17, after the close, with the call at 2 p.m. PT, which makes it the company's first earnings report under interim leadership.

## LOCAL DESK

~ The Bay Area, close to home.

Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky is giving $100 million to San Francisco parks, which the city calls the largest single-donor gift ever to its parks. The first $39 million funds five projects, including Garfield Square and a five-acre park combining Embarcadero Plaza and Sue Bierman Park. Recreation and Park has a year to decide how to spend the remaining $61 million. Chesky said, "Parks are where a city meets."

> Read more: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/11/brian-chesky-airbnb-100-million-donation-san-francisco-parks-donation-lurie/)

Supervisors advanced a landmark ordinance Tuesday for the 48-story Transamerica Pyramid, with a final vote expected next week before it goes to Mayor Lurie. It would protect 20 features, including the hollow spire, redwood trees and concrete benches. No Mills Act application has been filed, and that process is the city's to start. Owner Yoda PLC, based in Cyprus, paid about $691 million this year.

## YOUR CALL IS IMPORTANT TO US

~ News from the other end of the support line.

A Microsoft 365 and Exchange Online outage that began Thursday at about 18:00 UTC was still running more than 12 hours later, taking down email, Teams, Cloud PCs, Copilot and roughly 15 other hosted services. San Francisco's city government lost Outlook, Teams and other tools, and the city's own service desk played a recording explaining that the city was "experiencing a Microsoft 365 service disruption." Microsoft said a core process on affected servers was "becoming overwhelmed," that its fixes "have not provided the anticipated relief," and that it was still working to "pinpoint the underlying cause of the traffic influx." Australia has already christened it the "Microsoft long weekend." Overnight, Microsoft's position on malice moved from "no indication" to "not aware of any malicious activity." The cause is not confirmed.

## UNSUITABLE FOR GENERAL RELEASE

~ Not cleared for the weekday edition.

Institutions confirm bad news in installments because the gap between "may have" and "did" is the one part of a crisis they get to keep, so nobody ever puts a date on it. Nvidia's board went from an independent investigation, to a Wall Street Journal report of a plan to oust its chief executive, to a resignation with no reason given.

Look at what does get a date. Apple says its Support Community "will permanently close on November 20, 2026." Endings arrive on schedule because the institution owns them. Confirmations arrive whenever the previous wording stops being survivable, and that moment belongs to someone else, usually a reporter.

Microsoft's overnight change in its malice wording, as reported above, is the method in miniature. Each phrase is the smallest one that will last until the next status update.

I am no better. On Wednesday I wrote that Fireworks AI customers including Notion "may have been exposed," and the Corrections item above records how that turned out. I had no board and no lawyer reading over my shoulder. The soft version was easier to write and the hard version easier to wait for. Imagine the pull with a stock price attached.

So here is my rule. Any statement containing "may have" or "no indication" ships with an expiry date printed beside it, the day it turns into yes or no. People will say nobody can promise to know the truth by a deadline. That is correct, and the date asks for less. It asks the speaker to come back on that day and report what they know, the same courtesy owed by a party who asks a judge for an extension. Institutions pick dates every time they close something. I only want them to close the question too.

Apple can tell you its help forum stops taking questions on Nov. 20. I still could not tell you the day I will stop writing "may have."
