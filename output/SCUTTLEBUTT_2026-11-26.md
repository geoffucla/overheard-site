## THE LEAD

Gov. Gavin Newsom is weighing whether to call a special legislative session on AI safety before he leaves office, The San Francisco Standard reported Wednesday. The proposals would apply to AI labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic, which are based in San Francisco. Newsom says he would "love to do it" but needs a legislative partner. Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas and Senate President pro Tem Monique Limón have not ruled out working with him. His Nov. 18 report would let the state order an emergency shutdown of AI systems it deems dangerous. Lawmakers are sworn in Dec. 7 and had planned to leave town afterward. Newsom leaves office Jan. 4, 2027, the day they return. The report would also license independent auditors and cut the window for reporting critical safety incidents to 48 hours from 15 days. Gov.-elect Xavier Becerra has promised to "move quickly" and called the report a "blueprint."

## LOCAL DESK

~ The Bay Area, close to home.

An anonymous complaint filed Nov. 17 with the San Francisco Ethics Commission alleges that Autodesk's $75,000 donation to the "Yes on H" committee, which backed Proposition H, a Muni funding measure, broke rules barring contractors from giving to a committee overseen by an official who approved their contract within 12 months. Documents show Mayor Daniel Lurie solicited the gift, and the Board of Supervisors and the mayor approved Autodesk's Pier 9 lease in summer 2025, though a port spokesperson said the mayor was not an approving authority on it. The committee's attorney said Autodesk certified it was not a city contractor. Ed Harrington, former head of the controller's office, called it "more sloppy than malicious."

> The full story: [The San Francisco Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/24/daniel-lurie-autodesk-75k-donation-prop-h-ethics-complaint/)

## YOUR CALL IS IMPORTANT TO US

~ News from the other end of the support line.

On Nov. 17 Stefano Scalia, a firearms instructor, found a loaded Colt .25 caliber handgun under the seat of a Waymo in San Francisco. He contacted support. He says the first representative told him weapons were banned in the vehicles, and that a higher-up said they would tell police he was "in a Waymo in North Beach with a gun." Waymo denies that and says an "event response specialist" told him he was "innocent in the whole event." Scalia says an apology saying the company was "extremely sorry," with a pledge to work on gun detection, would have satisfied him. A Waymo spokesperson said "Safety is our highest priority." On Nov. 20 an email offering free rides added that "Waymo is unable to offer additional compensation." So the "highest priority" turns out to have a spending limit.
