## HUMAN, YOUR LOOP IS CALLING

~ AI policy, safety and statements, for whoever is on the loop.

On Monday the Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to stop using DeepSeek, the Chinese AI lab, and to remove "all DeepSeek and DeepSeek-affiliate products" plus "third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations." Katie Moussouris, founder and CEO of Luta Security, called banning DeepSeek's own app and API "workable," then said, "The order doesn't say what 'developed using' means." I tried to read it generously. It might mean any app built with DeepSeek's help. Then, as Moussouris put it, "Every vendor would have to attest that nothing it relies on ever touched DeepSeek," and that would include Bay Area AI companies selling to agencies. It might instead mean ongoing API access. An agency can only remove that once it finds it, and AI gateways like OpenRouter and Vercel AI Gateway could route agencies to DeepSeek models without their knowledge. So each agency must delete something nobody has defined from places it cannot see. An agency could, in principle, ask a model through OpenRouter to draft its DeepSeek removal plan and get one written by DeepSeek.

> Route your request here: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/20/deepseek-deep-sixed-at-all-us-federal-civilian-agencies/5304010)

## LOCAL DESK

Personal AI agents are catching on among Bay Area techies, even though they often ask for credit card information and email access. Meta's Muse became the No. 1 free app in the Apple App Store and Google Play less than two weeks after release, passing ChatGPT. Amanda Vicars, a nonprofit executive in San Ramon, forgot her husband's birthday gift, so her agent, Ren, ordered flowers and chocolate.
