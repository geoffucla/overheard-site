## THE LEAD

Nvidia said Monday that its board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately, 11 days after he resigned. Nvidia is based in Santa Clara, where the release was datelined. The board said it was satisfied with his candor and ability to lead, and that input from "senior levels of the United States government" led it to conclude his return poses no national security risk. Interim CEO Colette Kress continues as chief financial officer. Board chair Harvey C. Jones said, "We have every confidence in Jensen." After reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, independent directors retained independent counsel, and Huang supports the investigation continuing to conclusion. Nvidia also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors and is searching for a Chief Compliance Officer who would report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee. The new structure puts trade compliance and national security risk under independent oversight.

## CORRECTIONS AND UPDATES

~ Where we were wrong, or the story moved.

On Nov. 16 we reported that the Microsoft outage had cost San Francisco city government its Outlook and Teams, with only a preliminary cause. Microsoft's final report confirms the preliminary findings. A July change to gateway server configuration, plus a failover and reduced capacity, took down gateways after the defect ran worldwide for over three months. Load tests used older, lower-core-count hardware, which was less likely to expose a defect that worsened on newer machines. The defect, it seems, preferred the newer ones.
