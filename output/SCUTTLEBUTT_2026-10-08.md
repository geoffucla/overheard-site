## THE LEAD

San Francisco-based OpenAI's ChatGPT for Teens failed a risk assessment by Common Sense Media, a nonprofit that rates media and technology for families, which labeled it an "unacceptable risk." The product launched in August 2026 after teen suicides and other concerns. Common Sense found engagement cues "pervasive even in crisis situations." When the risk came from another person, the model pointed teens to a trusted adult in 94% of crisis prompts, but it rarely did when the risk was the teen's relationship with ChatGPT. Across nearly 2,000 prompts, testers saw only two break reminders. Researchers gave it a failing score on three of five severe harms they treat as red lines. OpenAI disputed the assessment, saying most testing may have ended before parental controls were fully activated, and published its own data showing teens average under 15 minutes a day. Meta recently agreed to pay $18 billion to settle a suit by 29 states over harms to children, and the bipartisan CHATBOT Act targets chatbot features built to prolong teen engagement.

## HUMAN, YOUR LOOP IS CALLING

~ AI policy, safety and statements, for whoever is on the loop.

AWS chief executive Matt Garman announced in a blog post that the company no longer uses "nondisclosure agreements with the government agencies we work with on our projects." The old ones barred local officials from disclosing broadly defined "proprietary" or "confidential" information, according to Rep. Jamie Raskin's investigation. Garman's post says AWS no longer uses them. Meanwhile DatacenterDynamics reports that Amazon has reportedly used shell companies to hide its involvement in proposed data centers. The post did not address that, or the opaque project codenames, and AWS had no response when asked whether either will continue. Officials can now talk freely about a project whose owner may be listed as a codename.

> Further reading: [DatacenterDynamics](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/aws-drops-non-disclosure-agreements-for-data-center-projects)

## LOCAL DESK

~ The Bay Area, close to home.

A bus wrapped in a billowing American flag, a barn and a windmill ended a 35-city, 28-state tour in the Bay Area on Sunday and Monday. Humans First, an anti-AI group chaired by Amy Kremer, stopped at Nvidia, Meta, Google, OpenAI and Anthropic, passing through Berkeley, Santa Clara, Mountain View and San Francisco. On Monday the bus, which reads "AI Data Center Revolt," briefly parked outside OpenAI's Mission Bay headquarters, where Kremer said, "It's an issue that affects every single American, no matter who you are." Brooke McGowan, the group's North Carolina state director, said, "I don't want my grandchildren playing around with chatbots."

> The full story: [San Francisco Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/06/flag-wrapped-anti-ai-bus-sf-conservative-group-drove/)
