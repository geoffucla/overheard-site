## UNSUITABLE FOR GENERAL RELEASE

~ Not cleared for the weekday edition.

I have come to believe that the only deliverable a technology company can still promise with confidence is an ending. Fixes slip and features break, but a retirement date arrives on time, every time, so institutions have learned to announce the date and skip the remedy.

Consider the week's purest specimen. Microsoft will retire its To Do apps on June 30, 2027, with no automatic migration, and points users to Planner, which lacks list groups and desktop apps. Notice what the announcement commits anyone to doing. Nothing, really. The calendar does the work, and the calendar has never once missed a deadline.

Compare that with the alternative. When the Microsoft 365 email outage stretched past 12 hours, it did so while the fixes kept failing. A fix rests on engineers. A date rests on the rotation of the Earth. If your bonus rested on keeping promises, which kind would you make more of?

The date also performs a quiet transfer. Before it is announced, the problem belongs to the company. Afterward it belongs to you, with a deadline. The Apple Support Community closes today, as scheduled, and the people who spent years answering each other's questions there are left holding the final one, which is where to go next.

The obvious objection is that a date beats silence, and I agree. A date is honest about the end and says nothing about the middle.

So I propose one rule. Every retirement date must arrive with a remedy date beside it, on the same page, in the same type size. Until then I take my comfort where I can. A to-do list with a fixed end date and nothing carried over is, I suppose, the first one in history that actually got finished.
